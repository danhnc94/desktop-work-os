# Singapore Market Entry

Use this reference to create research plans and market-entry logic for the interior design materials platform.

## Strategic Hypothesis

Singapore is the best first overseas pilot because the parent interior design company can create initial demand-side pressure through its own design projects, while existing supplier relationships create a starting pool for the supply side.

The first phase should validate whether the platform can connect:
- Supplier materials.
- Designer selection/specification behavior.
- Project demand.
- Platform content and data readiness.
- Supplier belief in ROI.

## Research Questions

### Market Demand

- How many Singapore or Singapore-linked design projects can realistically use the platform in the next 90 days?
- Which project types matter most: residential, commercial, hospitality, retail, office, luxury?
- What material categories are most frequently specified?
- Who influences material selection: designer, project manager, client, procurement, contractor, supplier rep?

### Supplier Motivation

- What do suppliers currently do to get specified by designers?
- How do they evaluate a new platform channel?
- What would count as ROI in the first 3 months?
- Does the local branch or distributor have authority to join?
- Who owns product data, images, certificates, pricing, and samples?

### Designer Adoption

- Are designers required to use the platform for overseas projects?
- What would make designers actually search/select materials there?
- What categories need to be available before the platform feels useful?
- What current workflow would the platform replace or support?

### Internal Readiness

- Who owns supplier onboarding?
- Who owns product content quality?
- Who owns designer adoption?
- Who fixes platform friction?
- Who reports pilot progress to CEO?

## Interview Guides

### Supplier Interview

Use Chinese internally; use English externally if speaking with Singapore suppliers.

English questions:
- `What would make this platform worth your team's time in the first 90 days?`
- `Who in your organization would need to approve onboarding?`
- `What product information is already available, and what would be difficult to prepare?`
- `How do you currently get your materials specified by designers?`
- `What kind of project visibility or designer engagement would you consider valuable?`
- `Would a simplified pilot onboarding path make participation easier?`

### Designer Interview

- Which material categories are most urgent for Singapore projects?
- How do you currently discover and compare materials?
- What makes you trust a supplier or product?
- What platform friction would stop you from using the library?
- What minimum supplier coverage would make the platform useful?

### Internal Team Interview

- What support can product realistically provide in the next 30 days?
- What content standards are mandatory versus nice-to-have?
- Which overseas team members can help supplier follow-up?
- What internal KPI currently supports or blocks this work?

## Competitors And Alternatives

Treat competitors broadly, not only digital platforms:
- Existing offline supplier relationships.
- Distributor sales teams.
- Direct manufacturer catalogues.
- Designer sample libraries.
- Material showrooms.
- Procurement-approved vendor lists.
- Generic search, PDFs, WhatsApp/email workflows.

Ask:
- What is faster than the platform today?
- What is more trusted than the platform today?
- What must the platform uniquely provide?

## 30/60/90-Day Validation

### First 30 Days

Validate:
- Supplier willingness.
- Main objections.
- Minimum onboarding requirement.
- Priority material categories.
- Internal ownership.

Outputs:
- Supplier segmentation.
- Interview findings.
- Pilot supplier shortlist.
- Minimum content standard.
- CEO update.

### First 60 Days

Validate:
- Supplier onboarding conversion.
- Content workflow speed.
- Designer search/selection behavior.
- Product friction.

Outputs:
- First live supplier cohort.
- Usage signal report.
- Friction backlog.
- Revised supplier pitch.

### First 90 Days

Validate:
- Whether the model creates enough supplier and designer value to scale.
- Which categories and supplier types should be prioritized next.
- Whether the team structure and platform process can support expansion.

Outputs:
- Pilot readout.
- Scale/stop/pivot recommendation.
- Next-market criteria.
- Updated resource ask.
